NEXUS

A complete, from-scratch design for a society where power cannot concentrate, money is out of governance, and every person keeps their freedom. Built from proven parts. Untested parts flagged, never hidden.

The Principles

  1. Power must be structurally impossible to concentrate — no single point of control.
  2. Money influence eliminated from governance.
  3. Every human has equal access to food, shelter, education, and health.
  4. Labor and contribution replace currency as the measure of value.
  5. Decentralized by default — no grouping that recreates pyramid authority.
  6. Transparent by design — all decisions public, traceable, immutable.
  7. A happy, sane, working model that preserves freedom. No 40-hour mandatory slave job.
  8. Voluntary association — groups may form for business and services, never imposed.

In Short

1

A power-decentralizing, money-out-of-governance system is buildable from proven parts, not utopian invention. Every NEXUS component maps to a real working precedent: sortition (Ostbelgien's permanent Citizens' Council; the Athenian Boule of 500), contribution economics (Mondragon's €5.02bn worker-owned federation; WIR Bank's ~1.5bn CHF/yr mutual credit), universal services (Costa Rica's 81-year life expectancy at $1,935/capita), and restorative justice (Norway's ~20% two-year recidivism). The novel work is assembling them.

2

The hard limits are real and must be designed around, not wished away. Worker cooperatives still fail in markets (Fagor, 5,600 jobs at risk, 2013); local currencies die when they over-rely on one champion (Ithaca Hours); collectives bureaucratize and were forced to re-decentralize (Zapatistas, 2023); and tech-funded charter states reliably recreate oligarchy (Próspera). NEXUS keeps the mechanisms that survived stress and discards the ones that didn't.

3

Transition is gradual and municipal, not revolutionary. The realistic path layers parallel institutions onto existing cities (Porto Alegre, Kerala, Ostbelgien) over 2026 → 2030 → 2050. Clean-sheet new states (network states / seasteads) are flagged as the highest-risk, most capture-prone route and are explicitly rejected.

The Blueprint

Ten scope items. Each leads with real-world evidence, then the NEXUS design, then flagged assumptions.

Key Findings

01

Sortition works and is already institutionalized.

The OECD Deliberative Database holds 733 representative deliberative cases from 1979 to 2023 across 34 countries (96% within OECD members); at least 80,622 citizens have been randomly selected since 1979. Ostbelgien, Belgium has run a permanent, law-mandated Citizens' Council since 2019. This is the strongest empirical foundation in the blueprint.

02

Money can be structurally separated from agenda-setting.

Combine randomly-selected deliberative bodies (no campaigns to fund) with radical transparency and rotation. The failure mode to avoid is captured decentralization — Próspera-style network states concentrate power in investors.

03

Universal basic SERVICES beat universal basic INCOME.

Costa Rica and Cuba show world-class health outcomes at a fraction of US per-capita spend. Services are harder to capture and inflate than cash.

04

Contribution can partially replace currency, but not entirely.

Mutual credit (WIR, Sardex) and time-banking work as complements, not total replacements. Every pure local currency studied either stayed marginal or collapsed.

05

A shorter work week is empirically safe.

Iceland (86% of workforce now on shorter hours) and the UK 2022 trial (92% of firms continued) show productivity holds at 4 days / 35–36 hours. The no-40-hour-slave-job principle is the best-evidenced demand in the document.

06

Restorative justice raises satisfaction and cuts general recidivism — but not violent recidivism.

So NEXUS cannot be purely abolitionist about violent crime; it pairs restorative defaults with rehabilitative containment.

07

Collectives that scale tend to bureaucratize or get crushed.

Catalonia 1936, the Paris Commune, Yugoslav self-management, and the Zapatistas all either fell to force or had to fight internal pyramid-formation. Anti-capture must be engineered in from day one.

Scope 01

Governance Model

Real-world evidence

  • OECD deliberative wave: 733 representative deliberative cases 1979–2023 across 34 countries; assemblies gather on average 90 people for 18.8 days over an 18-month span. Environment and long-term issues dominate (32% of 2023 cases) — sortition handles exactly the long-horizon problems elected politicians dodge.
  • Ostbelgien (Belgium, since 2019): world's first permanent, law-mandated sortition body. A 24-member Citizens' Council (one-third rotated every 6 months over an 18-month term) sets the agenda and convenes 1–3 Citizens' Assemblies/year (25–50 members). Open to residents 16+, including non-citizens; participants paid. A region under 80,000 people with its own legislative powers.
  • Ireland Citizens' Assemblies (2016–2018): directly produced the referendums legalizing same-sex marriage and abortion — deliberation resolving issues that paralyzed elected politics for decades.
  • Athenian sortition (~508–322 BCE): most magistracies filled by lot via the kleroterion (a randomizing machine to deter bribery); Council of 500 chosen annually by lot, one-year terms, no immediate re-election; pay to serve so the poor could participate. Generals were elected, not sorted — a deliberate exception for technical competence. Ended by conquest, not internal decay.
  • Haudenosaunee Great Law of Peace: Grand Council of 50 sachems; Clan Mothers nominate and can depose chiefs; consensus across nations; the seventh-generation principle. Founding date contested (1142 AD via eclipse evidence vs academic c. 1450–1570).
  • Quadratic voting: used in Taiwan's Presidential Hackathon and the Colorado legislature (2019). Captures preference intensity. Key vulnerability: needs strong identity/sybil resistance; Colorado's anonymous QV was found in a 2024 ruling to conflict with open-meetings law.

NEXUS design

Nested sortition councils plus mandated delegation.

  • Base unit: the Commune (a few dozen to ~150 households, Rojava model). Decisions at the lowest viable level; higher bodies coordinate, not govern (subsidiarity).
  • Agenda-setting by permanent sortition councils (Ostbelgien) at each scale; policy detail by ad-hoc citizens' assemblies (Ireland/OECD). Members paid, rotated, term-limited, no re-selection within a fixed window.
  • Delegates are mandated and recallable (Paris Commune + Rojava), not free representatives. Higher-scale bodies staffed by recallable delegates, never a standing political class.
  • Quadratic voting for budget/priority-setting with verified-personhood credentials and PUBLIC ballots (rejecting Colorado's anonymous version to preserve transparency).
  • Technical/operational roles (the generals): elected or competency-screened, never pure lot — the explicit Athenian exception.
  • Anti-capture, engineered: no standing executives; all coordination roles rotate and are recallable at any time; salary cap at a skilled worker's median wage (Paris Commune rule); all decisions public and append-only logged; sortition removes campaign money — the single biggest cut to money-in-governance.

Flagged assumptions & open questions

  • CLEAN-SHEET: no society has run nested permanent sortition at national scale. Ostbelgien is under 80,000 people. Scaling to millions is untested.
  • Mandated-delegate federations historically drift toward pyramid authority (the Zapatistas dissolved their Juntas de Buen Gobierno in 2023 precisely because the structure had become vertical/pyramidal). Recall is the only known countermeasure and it is imperfect.
  • QV at population scale depends on an unsolved identity/sybil problem.
Scope 02

Food System

Real-world evidence

  • Smallholder/peasant agriculture (La Via Campesina, founded 1993; 182 organisations in 81 countries; ~200 million people). Widely cited: peasant agriculture feeds ~70% of people on ~25% of resources (ETC Group). Contested: other research finds farms under 2 hectares grow ~34% of food on ~12% of arable land — still markedly more land-efficient. FAO: smallholdings are ~80% of the world's farms.
  • CSA, urban/vertical farming: vertical farming carries high energy/capex costs; economics unproven at staple-crop scale.
  • Regional food councils, permaculture at scale lack large rigorous datasets — mostly local case studies.

NEXUS design

Subsidiarity in food — hybrid sovereignty plus regional backstop.

  • Default: local/bioregional food sovereignty (Via Campesina) — communities control seeds, land, production.
  • Regional federated food councils coordinate surplus/deficit balancing and logistics across communes — the missing piece in pure localism.
  • Staples backstopped regionally; perishables and specialty local. Mirrors the land-efficiency of smallholders while accepting that not every region is self-sufficient in everything.

Flagged assumptions & open questions

  • The 70% / 25% figure is contested; NEXUS cites the conservative version (34% / 12%) to stay defensible.
  • No large-scale modern example of a fully food-sovereign industrial society exists. Cuba's Special Period agroecology is the closest, and was born of crisis.
Scope 03

Labor System

Real-world evidence

  • Mondragon (Spain, 1956–): world's largest worker-cooperative federation. 2024: 70,085 employees; industrial co-ops alone posted €5.020bn sales, €267m net income, 73% international sales. One-person-one-vote; capped pay ratios.
  • Failure mode — Fagor (2013): the original cooperative went bankrupt with €1.1bn debt, 5,600 jobs at risk; ~1,710–2,000 worker-owners were relocated via the intercooperation principle (a safety net capital firms lack). Governance lesson: councils of worker-owners had to approve multi-billion-euro foreign acquisitions; assembly attendance was low except on pay votes. By ~2019 only about one-third of employees were owner-members.
  • 4-day week: Iceland (2015–2019, 2,500+ workers; productivity maintained or improved; led to 86% of the workforce gaining shorter hours). UK 2022 (61 companies, ~2,900 workers): 56 continued (92%), 18 made it permanent, 71% reported reduced burnout, revenue rose 1.4% on average.
  • Time-banking / commons peer production: functional but small-scale.

NEXUS design

Contribution-based, voluntary, capped.

  • Work organized through voluntary worker cooperatives and commons projects (Mondragon structure) — never imposed, never top-down.
  • Adopt Mondragon's intercooperation safety net (relocate/retrain rather than lay off) and its pay-ratio caps — but discard the two-tier owner/non-owner split (every worker is a member from day one).
  • Standard contribution week ≤ 32–36 hours (Iceland/UK evidence), explicitly rejecting the 40-hour mandate.
  • Split labor into essential (rota-shared, guaranteed) and voluntary (self-directed). Care work counts as essential contribution.

Flagged assumptions & open questions

  • Mondragon survives by competing in a capitalist market. A whole economy of co-ops with no external market discipline is untested — the central open question of the labor model.
  • Large co-ops (>3,000 members) suffer democratic deficits (Fagor). NEXUS caps direct-democratic firm size and uses delegate assemblies above it — borrowed from Mondragon's own post-mortem.
Scope 04

Currency or No Currency

Real-world evidence

  • WIR Bank (Switzerland, since 1934): ~60,000 business members; ~1.5bn CHF/yr turnover (~1–2% of Swiss GDP); counter-cyclical (usage rises in downturns). Centrally issued and collateral-backed — not pure mutual credit.
  • Sardex (Sardinia, since 2009): B2B mutual credit, 1 Sardex = €1 but non-convertible; ~4,000 businesses, ~€33–40m/yr trades; credit limits ~1% of turnover, positive-balance cap ~10%; golden rule — only spend what you expect to earn back.
  • Ithaca Hours (1991–~2010s): time-based local currency, 1 Hour = $10; several million dollars traded among 500+ businesses. Why it died: over-reliance on founder Paul Glover (who left town), failure to digitize as cash declined, directory backlogs. The canonical single-point-of-failure lesson.
  • UBI vs UBS: Widerquist's net cost for a US UBI of $12k/adult + $6k/child is ~$539bn/yr (~3% of GDP), not the ~$3tn gross figure. Critics counter gross programs cost ~$2.4–3.8tn and could shrink labor supply. Universal Basic Services (UCL IGP, 2017) is the alternative.

NEXUS design

No single currency; layered value-tracking.

  • Universal Basic Services (not UBI) as the floor — food, shelter, education, health provided directly. Harder to capture/inflate than cash.
  • Mutual credit (Sardex/WIR) for inter-commune and business exchange — interest-free, balance-capped, non-hoardable.
  • Contribution credits / time-banking for labor recognition — explicitly NOT a savings/wealth instrument. Mutual credit breathes, expanding and contracting with capacity and trust.
  • Reject the pure moneyless resource-based economy (Venus Project) as untested and lacking any mechanism to allocate scarcity.

Flagged assumptions & open questions

  • Every pure local/time currency studied stayed marginal or collapsed. NEXUS's bet that layered systems plus UBS avoid this is clean-sheet.
  • Ithaca's lesson: any value-tracking layer must be digital-native and must not depend on a single champion.
Scope 05

Education System

Real-world evidence

  • Finland: no standardized tests until ~16; teachers hold master's degrees, only top applicants admitted; high autonomy; topped early PISA. Caveat: PISA scores have declined since the early-2000s peak, and immigrant-background students score lowest in the OECD — the model is not flawless.
  • Sudbury/democratic schools, unschooling: self-directed; smaller evidence base, generally positive socialization/autonomy outcomes but selection-biased samples.
  • Open courseware, peer-to-peer, guild/apprenticeship: mature models with strong informal track records.

NEXUS design

Free, decentralized, skill-based and lifelong.

  • Finnish principles: teacher autonomy, equity, minimal high-stakes testing, education as a public good.
  • Democratic/Sudbury self-direction at primary level plus guild/apprenticeship pathways for vocational mastery.
  • Lifelong open courseware plus peer learning funded as a Universal Basic Service.

Flagged assumptions & open questions

  • Finland's success rests on high-status, master's-trained teachers and a high-trust society — both take decades to build and may not transfer.
  • Fully decentralized unschooling lacks large rigorous outcome data; NEXUS retains a common-standards backstop.
Scope 06

Health System

Real-world evidence

  • Costa Rica: per the OECD's Health at a Glance 2025, life expectancy was 81 years (0.1 below the OECD average) while spending $1,935 per capita — vs the OECD average of $5,967 (USD PPP) — equal to 6.8% of GDP vs 9.3% on average. Life expectancy rose from 75 in 1990. EBAIS community-health teams deployed rural-first; preventive and determinants-focused.
  • Cuba: ~$813–$1,000/capita; life expectancy roughly equal to the US; lower infant mortality; prevention plus mandatory vaccination. Caveats: achieved under an authoritarian regime; low physician pay and supply shortages; health spending ~14% of GDP in 2021 — strong outcomes-per-dollar on absolute spend, not cheap as a GDP share.
  • Community health workers drive the outcomes-per-dollar advantage in both systems.

NEXUS design

Universal, preventive, non-profit, community-first.

  • Beveridge-style universal provision as a Universal Basic Service, free at point of use.
  • Costa Rica's EBAIS community-health-team model as the primary-care backbone; preventive funding prioritized over curative.
  • Remove profit from delivery; preserve innovation via publicly-funded research plus open licensing — de-link R&D reward from monopoly pricing.

Flagged assumptions & open questions

  • Removing profit from pharma R&D without killing innovation is partially untested — public/open-science models exist but haven't replaced the patent system at scale.
  • Cuba's outcomes are entangled with authoritarian coercion; NEXUS must achieve prevention through trust, not compulsion.
Scope 07

Justice System

Real-world evidence

  • Restorative justice meta-analyses: Latimer, Dowden & Muise (2005; 35 programs) found RJ more effective than traditional justice on victim satisfaction, offender satisfaction, restitution compliance, and recidivism. Fulham et al. (2025; 27 studies) found significant but SMALL reductions in general recidivism, NO significant reduction in violent recidivism, and large gains in victim satisfaction (d ≈ 0.63–0.65).
  • Norway: 2018 reconviction rate 18% within two years, 25% after five years, down from a 1980s baseline of ~60–70% after the shift to rehabilitation/normalization. Incarceration ~49–75/100,000 vs ~639–707 in the US. Maximum sentence 21 years (extendable). Norway spends ~$127,671/yr per inmate vs ~$25,000 in the US — humane but expensive. (Note: the ~20% figure is more narrowly defined than US rearrest rates.)
  • Indigenous/Gacaca/peacemaking circles: strong for community repair; Rwanda's Gacaca processed mass post-genocide caseloads with mixed due-process reviews.

NEXUS design

Restorative default, rehabilitative containment for violence.

  • Community restorative circles as the default for most harm — the satisfaction and general-recidivism evidence is strong.
  • Norway-style rehabilitative secure facilities for violent offenders — because RJ does NOT reliably reduce violent recidivism, NEXUS is explicitly NOT abolitionist about violent crime.
  • No profit in incarceration; reintegration-focused; normalization principle.

Flagged assumptions & open questions

  • RJ's recidivism effect is small and motivation-confounded (voluntary participants self-select). NEXUS should not over-claim.
  • Norway's model is expensive and culturally embedded; cost per inmate may not scale to poorer regions.
Scope 08

Transition Plan

Real-world evidence

  • Porto Alegre participatory budgeting (1989–): spread to ~17,000+ programs in 71 countries. Brazilian PB municipalities shifted spending to sanitation/health and saw infant mortality fall ~20% (Gonçalves, World Development, 2014); Porto Alegre raised water/sewage access from 75% to 98% in nine years. The best-evidenced bootstrap-from-inside mechanism.
  • Kerala People's Plan (1996–): devolved ~35–40% of the state development budget to local governments; literacy >96%, life expectancy ~75, infant mortality 7/1,000 (vs India's 28); declared free of extreme poverty in November 2025. Persisted across hostile central governments via social mobilization.
  • Rojava/AANES (2012–): ~5 million people; communes as base unit; 70% of budget to regions, 30% to higher structures; functional for a decade+ under war and embargo. But: academic critique finds higher bodies are NOT actually subordinate to communes as designed — pyramid drift again.
  • Network states / charter cities (Próspera): drew ~$50–120m from tech investors; governed by an appointed board plus foreign-judge arbitration, privatized services. Honduras revoked ZEDE status (2022), prompting an ~$11bn lawsuit. Charter-city originator Paul Romer called it a libertarian fantasy. The anti-model — decentralization that recreates investor oligarchy.

NEXUS design

Parallel municipal institutions, dual power, gradual.

  • 2026–2030 (seed): layer NEXUS bodies onto existing municipalities — participatory budgeting (Porto Alegre), local sortition councils (Ostbelgien), decentralized planning (Kerala). Start co-ops (Mondragon) and mutual-credit circuits (Sardex). No secession.
  • 2030–2040 (parallel power): federate communes regionally; expand UBS pilots; interlink mutual-credit networks. NEXUS institutions handle more real allocation as legitimacy grows.
  • 2040–2050 (transition): sortition bodies gain binding (not merely advisory) authority where mandated; UBS becomes primary provision.

Flagged assumptions & open questions

  • CLEAN-SHEET: no example exists of this fully completing. Kerala and Porto Alegre are the furthest-along partial cases, both inside existing states.
  • Dual power historically triggers state repression (Catalonia 1936 crushed; Paris Commune ~20,000–30,000 killed). Gradual municipal embedding is chosen specifically to avoid that confrontation.
  • The network-state/seasteading route is rejected as capture-prone.
Scope 09

Budget Models

Real-world evidence

  • UBI net cost: ~$539bn/yr (~3% of US GDP) for $12k/adult + $6k/child (Widerquist), vs ~$2.4–3.8tn gross. Finland's pilot was judged impossibly expensive at national scale by SAK's chief economist.
  • Universal healthcare per capita: Costa Rica $1,935 (6.8% of GDP) for ~81-yr life expectancy; Cuba ~$813–1,000; OECD avg ~$5,967 (9.3%); US ~$14,570 (2024). The outcomes-per-dollar case for universal/preventive systems is overwhelming.
  • Education: Finland achieves top outcomes with less testing infrastructure and moderate spend; high return on teacher quality.
  • Mutual credit: WIR's ~1.5bn CHF/yr at ~1–2% of GDP shows complementary systems can carry meaningful volume self-funded; Sardex unlocked ~€40m/yr of trade that stayed with small businesses and wouldn't have happened in euros.

NEXUS design

Fund UBS first; the contribution economy balances the rest.

  • Prioritize Universal Basic Services over UBI — better outcomes-per-dollar, harder to capture.
  • Health: target the Costa Rica frontier (~$2,000/capita range, community-team-led, preventive).
  • Finance transition via redirected existing welfare spend (net cost « gross), progressive/wealth/resource taxation, and mutual credit unlocking latent capacity.

Flagged assumptions & open questions

  • A contribution economy's macro-balancing — matching labor to needs without price signals — is the deepest unsolved problem. No large modern economy has done it. NEXUS keeps mutual-credit price-like signals precisely to avoid pure central planning's failures (Yugoslav shortages).

Caveats, Sources & Method

The three genuinely clean-sheet components

  • Nested sortition at national scale.
  • A contribution economy that macro-balances without prices.
  • Profit-free pharma innovation.

These are the blueprint's biggest risks and are labeled experimental, not solved.

Real-world evidence

  • The deepest unsolved problem is allocation without prices. Yugoslavia's shortages and the absence of any working moneyless economy mean NEXUS deliberately retains mutual-credit price-like signals rather than attempting pure planning. The Venus Project / resource-based economy is rejected as untested.
  • History's clearest warning is pyramid drift and armed suppression. Every scaled collective either bureaucratized (Zapatistas, Rojava, Yugoslavia) or was crushed by force (Catalonia, Paris Commune). Recall, salary caps, rotation, and gradual municipal (non-secessionist) embedding are the specific countermeasures — but none is proven at scale.
  • Source quality: cooperative/anarchist case studies skew toward sympathetic sources; key figures were cross-checked against the OECD, academic journals, and reference works. Contested figures (smallholder 70%/25%, the Haudenosaunee 1142 founding date, RJ recidivism effects, Norway's narrowly-defined ~20% recidivism) are flagged as contested in-text.
  • Many successes are small or crisis-born: Ostbelgien (<80,000 people), Sardex (one island), Cuba/Costa Rica (specific political economies). Scaling and transfer are assumptions, not demonstrated facts.
